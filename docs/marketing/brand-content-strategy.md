# Brand Content Strategy

**Status:** Strategy, not a posting calendar. This document says what the ClouDonna brand should publish and why; it does not schedule posts, does not automate publishing, and does not reference any individual's personal social-media presence. See the critical privacy requirement in the Website Perfection & SEO Release Gate — this strategy is brand-led by design, not founder-led in its public voice, even though the company itself is accurately described elsewhere as founder-led.

## Why brand-led, not founder-led, content

A founder's personal thought leadership is a legitimate growth channel for many B2B companies, and nothing here forbids the founder from having one. But this document specifically describes what *ClouDonna, the company* should publish under its own name, because:

1. The current release has an explicit, overriding instruction not to make the founder publicly discoverable by name.
2. Brand-authored content ages and compounds better as a company asset — it survives a change in who is writing it, the same way the product's evidence is attributed to a type, not to whoever happened to enter it.
3. It keeps the content held to the same evidentiary standard the product itself enforces (see Content Pillars below) — a company voice is naturally more scrutinized for unsupported claims than a personal, informal one, which fits ClouDonna's own stated principles better.

## Content pillars

Each pillar maps to something ClouDonna can speak about with genuine, demonstrated authority — either because the product does it, or because its evidence/neutrality model gives it a real point of view other vendors structurally cannot take (a vendor-affiliated platform cannot credibly publish "why vendor neutrality matters" the way an actually-neutral one can).

1. **Enterprise AI** — grounded in the AI Use Case Portfolio's quadrant model; never a trend piece.
2. **Enterprise Data** — grounded in the vendor catalog's structural categories (data platform, hyperscale cloud, operational database, enterprise legacy, decision/ops intelligence) and why conflating them is a category error.
3. **Technology Investment Decisions** — grounded in the Business Case Engine's actual cost/benefit taxonomy.
4. **Decision Quality** — the Donna Score model's ten dimensions, confidence scoring, and what "explainable" structurally requires (if the reasoning can't be shown, the recommendation isn't made).
5. **Business Cases** — TCO/ROI/NPV/payback done correctly, including when NOT to trust a single blended number (see the Scenario/Sensitivity work — largest driver, largest uncertainty, the exact flip condition).
6. **Vendor Neutrality** — the independence rules themselves, and the structural (not promised) mechanisms that enforce them.
7. **Architecture vs. Economics** — the Executive Recommendation model's own insight: the architecture lens, the economic lens and the strategic lens can legitimately disagree, and pretending they don't is worse than showing the disagreement.
8. **Why Enterprise Technology Decisions Fail** — concentration risk, undisclosed assumptions treated as facts, a recommendation with no stated condition under which it would change.
9. **Evidence vs. Opinion** — the six-type evidence taxonomy itself, and the specific failure mode of a claim "sounding confident" standing in for it actually being backed by something.
10. **Decision Intelligence** — the category-definition pillar; everything else on this list is a specialization of it (same relationship as content backlog item #8 to the rest of that list).

## Format principles

- Every piece links back to a real, live product surface wherever one exists (Scenario Lab, Cost & Value Lab, Trust Center, the vendor catalog) rather than existing as a standalone argument with no product proof behind it.
- No piece asserts a specific vendor's capability as fact without the same evidence-type discipline the product itself uses — a brand blog post making an unsupported vendor claim would directly contradict the product's own independence rules.
- No piece is published under a named individual's byline, personal title, or personal photo. Byline is "ClouDonna" or omitted, consistent with the Organization-only JSON-LD on the site (no Person schema).
- No piece references, links to, or is cross-posted from any individual's personal social-media account.
- Claims about product maturity follow the same LIVE/PARTIAL/PLANNED discipline as the site itself (see the claim-accuracy section of the release report) — brand content is held to the same bar the public site now is.

## What this document deliberately does not do

It does not assign a publishing cadence, does not name a channel-by-channel distribution plan, does not draft specific posts, and does not set up any automation. Those are operational decisions for whoever runs this program, made with current context about capacity and priority — a content *strategy* document that also tries to be a content *calendar* goes stale faster than either would alone.
