# Typography — Editorial Principles

## The typeface, unchanged and correctly so

Geist Sans + Geist Mono. The archived system's reasoning (`system-v1-archive/03-typography.md`) — purpose-built interface legibility, real tabular figures, and specifically *not* Inter, "the single most common typeface in AI-product UI today" — holds without revision. A typeface this considered doesn't need reconsidering every time the brief gets more ambitious; it needs to be trusted to carry more weight.

## Editorial, not interface-default

The shift Project NOVA asks for isn't a new typeface — it's permission to use the existing one at editorial scale and editorial restraint. A magazine's opening spread runs one enormous headline and very little else on the page; most SaaS product pages run a medium headline surrounded by six competing UI elements. The homepage's opening chapter (`09-homepage-story.md`) should look closer to the former.

## Large headlines

A new step above the archived scale's `text-hero` (72px): `text-cinematic`, fluid from 88px to 112px depending on viewport, reserved for exactly one sentence per site — the homepage's opening line. Everything else in the type system stays at its existing, already-correct scale (`system-v1-archive/03-typography.md`'s twelve steps) — the editorial move is restraint about *where* the largest size appears, not inflation of the whole scale.

## Minimal text

Every screen is edited for word count before it's edited for wording. A rule, not a suggestion: if a sentence can be cut without losing a fact the reader needs, it's cut, even if it was well-written. The Decision Score's executive summary is capped at what fits without scrolling on a standard viewport — not because more couldn't be said, but because an executive artifact that requires scrolling to finish reading has already lost the "screenshot into a board deck" test.

## Excellent readability

Body copy never exceeds ~70 characters per line, line-height 1.6 (already correct in the archived scale), and — new for Project NOVA — every block of body text on the Cinematic register is checked against its Carbon or Obsidian background at the *specific* opacity it will render at, not assumed correct because it passed on a flat swatch. Readability is verified against the real material, not the idealized token.

## Executive confidence

Numbers are always `tabular-nums` wherever they need to compare against each other (already correct, restated because it matters most exactly where an executive is scanning a comparison). Headings never end on an orphaned single word (`text-wrap: balance`, already correct). The one new rule: **no more than one number competes for attention on any screen.** The Donna Score is the number. Everything else supporting it is a word, a bar, or a band — never a second large number implying a second, competing headline fact.

## What editorial does not mean

It does not mean serif type, decorative display faces, or literary flourish in the copy itself. ClouDonna's editorial register borrows scale and restraint from print, not ornament — Geist stays sans-serif, geometric, and calm at every size, including 112px. The largest word on the homepage should look like the same typeface family as the smallest label on a data table, because it is.
