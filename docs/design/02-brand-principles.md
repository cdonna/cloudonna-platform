# Brand Principles

Permanent, and each one tested against a real objection before being kept — a principle nobody could argue against isn't a principle, it's a platitude.

## Silence is premium

**The claim:** a screen with nothing extra on it reads as more expensive than a screen working hard to fill the space.
**The objection:** silence can also read as unfinished, or as a company that had nothing else to say.
**Resolution, kept:** silence is premium only when what remains is complete on its own — a single, well-evidenced number is confident; a single number with nothing around it explaining what it means is just empty. This principle governs *removal* after something is whole, never an excuse to ship less than whole.

## Motion explains

**The claim:** every animation earns its place by making a specific fact easier to understand faster.
**The objection:** some motion exists purely to signal quality — a slow, considered transition can communicate "this was crafted" even with nothing specific being explained.
**Resolution, kept, narrowed:** motion explains *or* motion marks a threshold (an opening frame settling into place, a result arriving) — two jobs, not one, both real, both named so "it felt considered" doesn't quietly become an excuse for decoration with no job at all.

## Whitespace creates confidence

**The claim:** generous space around content signals the company isn't hiding anything or rushing the reader.
**The objection:** whitespace can also read as evasive on a data-dense enterprise screen, where an executive wants density, not air.
**Resolution, kept, scoped:** whitespace creates confidence *around a decision*; density is trusted *within evidence*. The Decision Score gets air. The evidence table beneath it doesn't need to, and forcing air into it would cost the very density that makes it credible. This is `08-dashboard-philosophy.md`'s central rule, stated here as its brand-level source.

## Typography is hierarchy

**The claim:** the size, weight, and position of text alone should tell a reader what matters most, without color or iconography doing that job.
**The objection:** none, genuinely — this one survives unchanged. A system that needs color to establish importance has already lost the hierarchy argument at the type level.
**Kept, unqualified.**

## Depth creates focus

**The claim:** a subtle sense of foreground and background (via material, not literal 3D) pulls a reader's eye to what's closest, the way real depth-of-field does.
**The objection:** depth effects (blur, shadow, layering) are exactly the kind of thing that ages badly and reads as trend-following within a few years — the specific risk a permanent brand principle can't afford.
**Resolution, kept, re-scoped:** depth as a *structural* idea (near things are simpler and sharper, far things recede) is timeless — glassmorphism as a *specific technique* is not, and isn't what this principle commits to. `04-material-system.md` implements the structural idea without the trend.

## Remove everything unnecessary

**The claim:** the strongest edit is always the one that removes something, not the one that adds a flourish.
**The objection:** taken literally, this principle has no floor — it would eventually argue for removing content the product genuinely needs.
**Resolution, kept, bounded:** "unnecessary" means unnecessary to the decision being made on that screen, not unnecessary in general. Evidence is never unnecessary on a screen whose whole job is defensibility, however much simpler the screen would look without it. This principle edits presentation, never substance.

## What survived the challenge, and what it means

Five of six principles needed real scoping to survive contact with an honest objection; one (typography is hierarchy) didn't need any. That ratio is itself the point of this document — a set of brand principles that all survived unscoped would mean nobody had actually tried to break them.
