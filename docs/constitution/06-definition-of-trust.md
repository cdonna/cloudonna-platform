# The Definition of Trust

## What trust is

Trust is the state in which someone acts on your word without re-verifying it every time. It is not agreement, and it is not affection — a person can trust an answer they dislike, and distrust one they'd prefer to be true. Trust is specifically the decision to stop independently checking, because a track record has made checking feel unnecessary. Every time trust is extended, the truster is accepting a real, measurable risk on the truster's own behalf. This is why trust is valuable, and why it is expensive to earn honestly: it can only be extended by someone actually taking on that risk, and it can only be deserved by consistently proving the risk was well placed.

## How trust is earned

Not through a claim of trustworthiness — through a repeated pattern of claims that, when checked, held up. Earning trust requires being checkable in the first place: an answer that cannot be independently verified cannot be trusted in the sense that matters, only believed, which is a weaker and more fragile thing. Trust accumulates specifically through the gap between what was promised and what was delivered staying small, visibly, over enough instances that the pattern itself becomes the evidence.

## How trust is measured

Not by how many people say they trust you. By how much independent verification they have stopped doing, and how much responsibility they have become willing to hand over as a direct result. The truest measure of trust is what someone is willing to *stop checking* — a customer who used to review every recommendation line by line and now reviews only the summary has given a real, observable signal; a customer who still checks everything has not, whatever they say out loud.

## How trust is lost

Disproportionately, and asymmetrically to how it was earned. A hundred honest instances build trust slowly, in small increments. One demonstrated instance of self-interest overriding honesty — one moment where the truster discovers their risk was not actually well placed — erases a share of that accumulated trust far larger than any single honest instance built. This asymmetry is not a design flaw in how trust works. It is the entire reason trust is valuable in the first place: if it were symmetric, it would be nothing more than an average, and an average can't be relied on the way trust needs to be.

## How trust is rebuilt

Never by re-asserting trustworthiness. Assertion is exactly the currency that was just proven unreliable — using more of it to recover from the failure repeats the original mistake. Trust is rebuilt only by re-accumulating a track record from a lower starting point than before, publicly acknowledging the specific failure without minimizing it, and accepting that the rebuilding will almost always take longer than the original earning did, because the truster now has direct evidence that checking was warranted. There is no shortcut here that does not, itself, become the next thing that has to be trusted and might fail.

## Why this document is central to the company

Every other capability the company builds is worth exactly as much as the trust extended to it, and no more. A more accurate deterministic engine, a deeper evidence graph, a richer decision history — none of it matters to someone who has decided not to believe the output. This definition exists so that trust is never treated as a mood to be managed with better communication, but as a specific, earned, measurable, losable, and re-earnable state — governed by what actually happened, not by what was said about what happened.
