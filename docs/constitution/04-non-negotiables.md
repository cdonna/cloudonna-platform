# Non-Negotiables

A value describes what the company prefers. A non-negotiable describes what the company will not do, under any pressure, at any size, for any amount of money. There are five. A longer list would not be more disciplined — it would mean the company hadn't decided which ones actually matter most.

## 1. Independence

No party who stands to benefit from a specific answer may influence that answer — not by payment, not by relationship, not by ownership. This is the one non-negotiable every other commercial decision the company ever makes must be checked against first, because it is the one whose loss cannot be partially recovered. A company can rebuild a product. It cannot rebuild an answer it once sold.

## 2. Evidentiary honesty

The company will never assert more certainty than the evidence supports, and will never allow speed, persuasion, or a customer's preference for a firm answer to erode that discipline. An honest "we don't know yet" is always an acceptable answer. A confident answer the evidence does not support never is.

## 3. Historical integrity

A record of what was decided, once made, is not edited to reflect what would have been more convenient in hindsight. It may be corrected — a correction is a new, dated, attributed addition, never a silent rewrite. An institution that edits its own past has forfeited the right to be trusted about its present.

## 4. Human accountability

Every consequential, irreversible action traces to a specific, named, accountable person. No process, however capable, is permitted to make that action's origin ambiguous. If a future capability would make it unclear who is answerable for an outcome, that capability does not ship, regardless of what it can otherwise do.

## 5. Customer sovereignty over their own record

What a customer builds through this company — their history, their evidence, their record of judgment — belongs to them. It is never used against their interest, never sold without their explicit and specific consent, and never held hostage to secure a renewal or a payment. A company that would use a customer's own trust as leverage against them was never actually trustworthy in the first place, whatever it claimed at the time.

## Why five, and why these five

Every one of these, broken once, does not cost the company a customer or a quarter. It costs the company the thing the other four depend on to mean anything — the belief that this is a company whose word can be taken at face value. That belief, once lost, is not a marketing problem to be solved with better messaging. It has to be earned again from zero, by someone who may not be the same person who lost it.
