# Values

A value that never costs anything is a preference, not a value. Each entry below names the real tension it resolves and which side wins, every time, including when the other side would be easier or more profitable in the moment.

**Being right later over sounding right now.** When evidence is incomplete, the correct answer is to say so, even though a confident, incomplete answer is more comfortable to give and easier to sell. A hedge that reflects real uncertainty is not weakness; a false confidence that reflects none is not strength.

**A defensible no over an agreeable yes.** When a request would compromise evidence, independence, or the integrity of the record, the answer is no, regardless of who is asking or what is being offered in return. An agreeable yes that costs the company its credibility has always been the more expensive choice, even when the invoice arrives later.

**The record over the relationship.** If honoring a customer relationship would ever require quietly altering what actually happened, the record wins. This will occasionally cost a relationship. It will never be allowed to cost the record.

**Slow and real over fast and impressive.** A capability that is genuinely built is worth more than the same capability merely demonstrated — the difference is invisible in a demo and decisive in a customer's own audit.

**What was earned over what was claimed.** Standing in the world — trust, category position, reputation — is only real to the extent it was actually earned through verifiable behavior. Anything claimed ahead of being earned is a debt, not an asset, and every debt of this kind eventually comes due at the worst possible time.

**The person who bears the consequence over the person who benefits from the decision.** When a recommendation could benefit the party paying for it at the expense of the party who has to live with the outcome, the second party's interest governs. This is not altruism. It is the only version of the business that can be trusted by the people it's meant to serve.

**Humility about the future over confidence about the roadmap.** Plans are useful and will be wrong in ways not yet visible. Holding a plan loosely, while holding the principles above tightly, is the correct posture — the plan is allowed to be wrong; the values that judge the plan are not.

## What makes these operational, not aspirational

Each one names a real cost the company will actually pay for holding it. A values document with no cost attached to any entry has not described what the company values — it has described what the company would like to be true without having to give anything up for it.
