# Culture

Not perks, not slogans. Culture here means one thing: how people actually behave when no one senior is watching and the easy path and the right path have diverged.

## How decisions are made

The person closest to the evidence makes the call, not the person with the most seniority. Seniority earns the right to ask harder questions before a decision is made, never the right to overrule a well-reasoned answer with an unreasoned one. A decision is written down with its reasoning at the time it's made, not reconstructed afterward to sound better than it was.

## How disagreement works

Disagreement is surfaced early and specifically, not smoothed over to preserve comfort in the room. A disagreement that turns out to be correct is worth more to the company than agreement that turns out to be convenient. The test of whether disagreement is actually welcome is not whether people are invited to raise it — it's whether raising it ever visibly costs the person who raised it something. If it does, the culture has already failed, regardless of what anyone says about being "open to feedback."

## How excellence works

Excellence is judged against the hardest honest question a skeptic could ask, not against the easiest audience in the room. Work that would embarrass the company if a critical expert examined it closely is not finished, no matter how polished it looks from a distance. Excellence is not speed, and it is not busyness — it is the discipline of not shipping something until it would survive being taken apart by someone who wants to find its flaws.

## How humility works

Being wrong in public, promptly and specifically corrected, is treated as a sign of a healthy process, not a personal failure to be managed defensively. The company is more afraid of a mistake that gets minimized than one that gets found. No one's title exempts them from being asked to show their reasoning, including the founder's.

## How learning works

Every real failure produces a specific, written, unflattering account of what happened — not a sanitized retrospective designed to make the next conversation easier. What was learned is applied before it's forgotten, and it is never quietly re-learned a second time because the first lesson was too uncomfortable to keep around. An institution that keeps making the same mistake in a new disguise has not actually built a learning culture, whatever it calls its retrospectives.

## What this culture is not

It is not a culture of speed for its own sake, and it is not a culture of comfort. Both are common, both are pleasant to work inside, and neither one, by itself, produces a company anyone should trust with a consequential decision. This culture is built to produce people who would rather be right and uncomfortable than agreeable and wrong — and to make sure that trade is actually rewarded here, not just praised in principle while something else gets promoted in practice.
