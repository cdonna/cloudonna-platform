# Beliefs

Each belief below was tested against one question: would this still be true if the technology underneath it were entirely different? What follows is what survived.

**Confidence should be proportional to evidence, regardless of who or what produced the claim.** This is true of a person, an institution, or a machine, and it will remain true under every future technology capable of producing a confident-sounding answer. The source of a claim does not change what the claim owes the truth.

**A tool that decides is different in kind from a tool that informs.** Not different in degree — different in kind, because the moment a tool decides, accountability for the outcome becomes ambiguous in a way it never is when a person decides with the tool's help. This distinction does not depend on how capable the tool is. It depends on who answers for the result.

**Memory strengthens accountability; forgetting weakens it.** An institution that cannot recall why it did something cannot truly be held responsible for having done it — the record is not a convenience, it is the precondition for responsibility to mean anything at all, years after the fact.

**The scarcest resource in a complex decision is not information. It is verified truth.** Information has never been more abundant or cheaper to produce. Confidence that it deserves to be believed has not gotten any easier to earn. The gap between those two facts is where every version of ClouDonna's value has to live, regardless of what generates the information a decision-maker is drowning in.

**Independence, once sold, cannot be bought back.** A single instance of letting payment influence an answer does not cost a company one sale. It costs the company the meaning of every answer it has ever given and will ever give again, because a viewer can no longer tell which ones were honest.

**Judgment can be assisted. It should never be laundered.** Assistance makes a person's judgment better-informed. Laundering makes a decision appear to belong to no one — produced by a process opaque enough that responsibility for it dissolves. The difference is not subjective; it is whether a specific, named party remains answerable for the outcome.

**Complexity is acceptable. Concealed complexity is not.** A decision can be genuinely difficult, resting on evidence that is itself uncertain. What is never acceptable is presenting that uncertainty as if it weren't there, because the decision-maker, not the system that advised them, is the one who will answer for it.

**Trust compounds slower than capability, and breaks faster than either.** Any institution whose growth outpaces its trust has borrowed against something it cannot pay back on demand.

## What this list deliberately leaves out

Nothing about any specific technology, market, or product. A belief that requires a particular kind of software to remain true is not a belief — it's a feature, filed in the wrong place.
