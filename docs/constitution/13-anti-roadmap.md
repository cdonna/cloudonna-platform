# The Anti-Roadmap

A roadmap says what will be built, eventually, in some order. This document says what will not be built, ever, regardless of order, budget, competitive pressure, or how compelling the argument for an exception sounds in the moment. Nothing here is a matter of current priority. Sequencing decisions belong elsewhere and change constantly; this list does not.

**A mechanism to let payment influence a recommendation, in any form, however indirect.** Not a featured tier, not a boosted placement, not a "sponsored" label applied honestly to something that still moves the answer. If money can move the answer, the answer was never independent, regardless of what else is true about the company.

**A way to alter a historical record after the fact, invisibly.** Corrections are permitted. Silent edits are not. If a record can be quietly rewritten, it was never a record — it was a draft pretending to be permanent.

**Any feature whose purpose is to make the product seem more certain than the underlying evidence supports.** No confidence display, no interface pattern, no narrative technique will ever be used to manufacture an impression of certainty the evidence itself does not earn.

**A path for the product to take an irreversible action without a specific, accountable human choosing to let it.** No matter how reliable the underlying judgment becomes, no matter how much time has passed without an error, this boundary does not move with capability. It is not a temporary limitation waiting to be lifted.

**Any interface that simulates a relationship the system doesn't actually have with the person using it.** No pretense of friendship, familiarity, or personal investment the system is not capable of. Calm and competent, never performed intimacy.

**A revenue model that depends on the customer not fully understanding what they agreed to.** Every commercial term is explainable, plainly, to the person paying for it, without a lawyer required to find the catch.

**Using a customer's own trust in the company as leverage against them** — to prevent them from leaving, to extract a renewal, to discourage a complaint. Trust that can be used as a hostage was never actually trust; it was dependency wearing trust's name.

**Treating engagement, attention, or usage volume as a goal in its own right**, independent of whether more of it actually serves the customer's decision better. A company whose product is judgment should never optimize for how much time someone spends consulting it — the honest goal is always a better decision reached, not more time spent reaching it.

**Building a capability whose real purpose only becomes clear once it's already justified by something else already built** — accumulating quiet dependencies until a compromise becomes technically necessary rather than a choice anyone visibly made. If a future capability would only become defensible after several other things were built first, each individually plausible, that pattern itself is treated as a warning sign, not a normal path to somewhere useful.

## Why this list is short

A long list of permanent refusals would suggest the company expects to be tempted by many different things. The real temptations, honestly examined, mostly reduce to one: trading independence for growth, in some disguise. Every entry above is a specific costume that trade tends to wear. The list stays short because the underlying refusal is singular, and naming it once, clearly, protects more than naming its disguises exhaustively ever could.
