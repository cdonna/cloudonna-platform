# Category Playbook — Defining and Owning Enterprise Decision Intelligence

Definition and competitive analysis already exist in full (`docs/company/02-category-definition.md`, `19-category-ownership.md`). This document is the *playbook* — what to actually do, in what order, to go from "a category that could exist" to "the category ClouDonna owns."

## The sequence: earn the name before you say it

1. **Prove the wedge, silently.** Real evidence, real design partners, real defensible decisions — for the narrow technology-decision beachhead only. No category language in any external communication during this phase. This is where the company is today.
2. **Publish the evidence, not the ambition.** The first public category-adjacent content should be a real, specific, citable finding from real (anonymized, per `docs/company/07-customer-learning-system.md`) usage — "enterprises evaluating data platforms cite integration complexity as their most under-evidenced concern" — never "we're building a new category." Findings earn credibility; announcements spend it.
3. **Become the citation.** The specific, deliberate goal named but not yet acted on in the prior strategy pass (`docs/company/15-critical-review-v2.md`): the moment an architect says "ClouDonna's evidence shows..." in a meeting ClouDonna isn't in, the category has a name-brand leader, whether or not "Enterprise Decision Intelligence" is trademarked anywhere. This is the actual finish line for category ownership — not press coverage, not a Gartner-style quadrant of ClouDonna's own invention.
4. **Only then, name the category out loud.** Once the citation behavior above is real and repeatable, category language stops being aspiration and starts being description. `docs/company/14-founder-decisions.md` #2 already recommends holding this line; this playbook makes it a sequenced trigger, not just a deferred decision.

## What "owning" a category actually requires, concretely

- **A body of real, specific, defensible findings** — not a platform feature list. Category ownership is a research and evidence achievement before it's a product achievement.
- **A vocabulary customers adopt without being told to.** If "Decision Intelligence" becomes a phrase a customer uses unprompted in a board deck, the category is owned. If it only appears in ClouDonna's own marketing, it isn't yet.
- **A reference architecture other companies get compared against**, even informally — "does your tool do what ClouDonna does" is a stronger ownership signal than any market-share number.

## The two failure modes to actively defend against

- **Announcing the category before the evidence exists.** Covered above — the single most likely way to waste the opportunity, because a category claim that turns out to be premature doesn't just fail quietly, it makes the *next* attempt to claim it (by ClouDonna or anyone) harder, having already been shown hollow once.
- **Winning the wedge and never expanding past it.** The beachhead (technology decisions) is deliberately narrow (`docs/company/06-go-to-market.md`). A category isn't owned by dominating one narrow use case forever — the playbook has to include a real, evidenced trigger for when and how the category expands beyond technology decisions into the broader "enterprise decisions" ambition, not just an implicit hope that it happens. **That trigger, explicitly: category expansion begins only after the technology-decision wedge has produced real, citable outcome data** (`docs/company/10-three-year-roadmap.md`'s Year 2 exit criterion) — proof the model works in one domain is the prerequisite for credibly claiming a second one, never a parallel bet made before the first one is proven.

## What ClouDonna does not do to "own" the category

Buy the category name into existence through paid analyst placement, sponsor a self-serving "state of decision intelligence" report with no independent credibility, or claim category leadership in a fundraising deck before the citation behavior in step 3 above is real. Every one of these is available, cheaper, and faster than actually earning the position — and every one of them is exactly the kind of hollow authority (Gartner's own vendor-funded research model, `docs/company/19-category-ownership.md`) this category exists specifically to be a credible alternative to. A category leader that won the position the way the incumbents it's replacing won theirs has not actually replaced anything.
