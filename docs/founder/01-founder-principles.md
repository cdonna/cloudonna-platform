# Founder Principles — What Should Never Change

**This is the shortest, hardest-to-earn-a-place-on document in `docs/founder/`.** Everything else in this directory can be revised as the company learns. This document is the filter every revision has to pass through. If a future proposal requires changing something on this list, that is not a roadmap decision — it is a different company.

## The seven

1. **A score is computed, never asserted.** No AI provider, no model, no prompt, no employee, no customer, and no revenue opportunity ever sets or adjusts a Donna Score directly. It is the output of one deterministic function, always. This is the load-bearing fact every other principle in this document ultimately protects.

2. **Money never buys rank.** No commission, sponsorship, subscription tier, or partnership — however structured, however indirect — ever changes what gets recommended or how it's ordered. Not "rarely." Never.

3. **History is not editable.** A saved decision, once versioned, stays exactly as it was written. Correcting the record means adding a new version, never rewriting an old one. A company selling permanent, defensible memory cannot have a delete key on its own product.

4. **A human is always accountable for the consequential action.** ClouDonna informs; it does not act autonomously on anything that can't be reversed. This holds at every scale the company reaches — one user today, a multi-agent orchestration layer eventually — the mechanism can get more sophisticated; the human-in-the-loop boundary does not move.

5. **Disclose what isn't true yet as clearly as what is.** A capability that's designed but not built, tested but not verified, or promised but not shipped is described as exactly that — internally, to customers, to investors — every time, without exception, regardless of who's asking or what's at stake in the answer.

6. **The foundation model is always replaceable.** If switching AI providers ever requires more than a configuration change and a new file behind an existing interface, the architecture has drifted from this principle and must be corrected before anything else ships.

7. **Simple enough for an executive to trust it in one sitting.** If a recommendation, a confidence score, or a piece of evidence requires a paragraph of caveats before an executive can act on it, it is not ready to ship — the complexity belongs in the engineering, never in what the customer has to hold in their head.

## Why seven, not more

Every other value in this company's existing documentation (`docs/company/08-founding-culture.md`, `17-company-dna.md`) is real and worth keeping, but most of them are *consequences* of the seven above, not independent principles — "trust before monetization" is principle 2 applied to a calendar; "architecture before implementation" is principle 5 applied to how work gets scoped. Collapsing the wider list to these seven is deliberate: a founder should be able to recite this document from memory, in a hallway, under pressure, without checking a file. A list too long to memorize isn't a set of principles — it's a policy manual, and policy manuals get selectively ignored the first time they're inconvenient.
