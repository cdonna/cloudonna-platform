## New contradiction found: a third, unreconciled knowledge-graph design

The prior review named two competing "Sprint 6.2" documents. There is a **third, older instance of the same failure mode**, more serious because it involves core data-model architecture, not just planning prose:

- `docs/platform/enterprise-intelligence-graph.md` (Sprint 4) — a full node/edge catalog (`SUPPORTS`, `IMPLEMENTS`, `COMPETES_WITH`, `RECOMMENDED_FOR`...) built on Sprint 4's schema (`decision_sessions`, `recommendations`, `decision_scores`, `products`), explicitly self-declared as "the actual source of truth for this domain."
- `docs/sprint-6/13-knowledge-graph.md` — a **different 27-node ontology** (`Business Process`, `Solution Pattern`, `Claim`, `Lesson Learned`...) built on different table names, with no reference back to the Sprint 4 document at all.

These are not the same graph described twice. They use different node vocabularies, different edge semantics, and different foundational tables for overlapping concepts (a "Product" in one is not obviously the same row as a "Product" in the other, given `docs/sprint-6/18-persistence-schema.md`'s own disclosure that Sprint 6.1 diverged from Sprint 4's persistence assumptions in three separate ways already). **Recommendation: before Sprint 6.4 starts, one document must be explicitly retired or the two explicitly merged** — building Sprint 6.4 against `13-knowledge-graph.md` without resolving this risks quietly stranding real, considered design work in `enterprise-intelligence-graph.md` (the four missing-relationship findings and three missing node types it identified are real content, not filler) or, worse, building a graph that a future reader can't tell is the second attempt at the same thing.

## Self-critique: weaknesses in the prior `docs/company/` strategy pass

- **The System of Action was invented, not derived, and I said so at the time — but it hasn't been stress-tested.** `03-product-strategy.md` explicitly flagged this as a new fifth system not present in the manifesto. On rereading: it is the weakest-specified of the five. "Structured, trackable, human-executed follow-through" is a fine description but has almost no concrete product shape behind it today, unlike the other four which all map to real, at-least-designed capability. There is a real risk this system exists in the document set because the prompt asked for five systems, not because ClouDonna's own architecture independently produced a fifth one. Worth naming honestly rather than defending.
- **The three-year roadmap's Year boundaries are softer than they read.** `10-three-year-roadmap.md` presents Year 0/1/2/3 with specific exit criteria, which is good discipline — but the underlying assumption (that Year 1 → Year 2 transition tracks "knowledge graph becomes real") assumes Sprint 6.4 is a single, well-scoped project. `docs/sprint-6/27-sprint-6-4-implementation-plan.md`'s own "smallest production-suitable vertical slice" framing suggests even the *first* Sprint 6.4 slice is a multi-quarter undertaking on its own — the Year 1/Year 2 line may be optimistic by a wider margin than the document currently signals.
- **The commercialization gates document was honest about Gate 1 not being passed — but understated how far Gate 2 actually is.** `11-commercialization-gates.md` lists Gate 2 criteria (rate limiting live, RLS independently verified, audit logging, Sprint 6.3 tenancy, formal Explainability Layer) as if they're a checklist to work through. In reality several of these (a real knowledge graph beginning to replace the illustrative catalog) depend on the unresolved knowledge-graph contradiction above being settled first — Gate 2 has a hidden architectural prerequisite the gate document doesn't currently name.

## Weak assumptions, stated as fact, across the broader document set

- **"Vendor-neutral forever" is treated as a solved problem because the scoring engine has no commercial field.** True and real, but it only covers *direct* score manipulation. It does not, by itself, prevent a *catalog curation* bias — who gets added to the vendor catalog in the first place, and on what basis, is a judgment call with no structural safeguard at all today. A catalog that only ever includes vendors ClouDonna has a relationship with would be "neutral" by the existing structural test and biased in every way that matters. Worth a named safeguard, not just a scoring-formula one.
- **"The unauthenticated experience is a genuine GTM asset" is asserted without acknowledging its ceiling.** `06-go-to-market.md` and the final report both lean on this — correctly, it removes friction — but it also means the first real signal from a prospect is low-commitment by construction (no email captured, no persistence, nothing to follow up on unless the conversation happens live). This is a real asset for a live founder-led conversation and a much weaker one for any GTM motion that isn't founder-present.
- **"Real customers will validate the ICP" is stated as if validation is symmetric.** It's more likely that early design partners over-index on whichever specific decision type they happen to be facing at the moment they're recruited, which is a sampling bias the customer-learning framework (`07-customer-learning-system.md`) doesn't currently correct for — three design partners each mid-way through a data-platform RFP will confirm the beachhead whether or not it's actually the strongest wedge, simply because that's who was recruited.

## Too ambitious

- **The five-system model applied uniformly to a pre-revenue company.** Real, coherent architecture — but `13-company-architecture.md`'s master diagram gives System of Learning and System of Action equal visual weight to System of Intelligence and Record, when by every other document's own honest maturity assessment they are 1–3 years further from being real. A diagram used in an investor or early-customer conversation risks implying parity that doesn't exist yet.
- **Naming a "10-year ambition" and a "three-year roadmap" while Sprint 6.1 sits uncommitted.** Not wrong to think that far ahead — genuinely useful — but worth the founder consciously deciding how much of that horizon to say out loud to an early prospect versus keep as internal orientation, since the gap between the stated horizon and the actual current state is the single easiest thing for a skeptical CIO or investor to use to discredit the whole pitch if it's presented without the honest caveats this document set otherwise insists on.

## Not ambitious enough

- **The Action system is under-imagined precisely where it could be most differentiated.** Every competitor category named in the founder questions (Gartner, Accenture, comparison portals) stops at the recommendation. A genuinely ambitious System of Action — not autonomous, but a structured, evidenced *implementation playbook* generated from the same deterministic reasoning that produced the recommendation — is a real, large, currently almost-unaddressed opportunity, and the existing documents treat it as an afterthought (`03-product-strategy.md`'s own "currently negligible" self-assessment) rather than a second wave worth deliberately building toward.
- **The category-ownership ambition doesn't yet claim the thing that would make it hardest to dislodge: becoming the citation.** Bloomberg's real moat isn't the terminal, it's that "Bloomberg says" became a citable, board-acceptable authority. Nothing in the current document set explicitly targets *becoming the reference enterprises cite to each other and to their boards* as a deliberate goal with its own workstream — it's implied by the category ambition but never named as a thing to actively build toward (content, benchmarks, public research) the way `14-founder-decisions.md`'s "research brand" decision gestures at but doesn't commit to.

## Duplicated ideas, beyond what was already flagged

- **"Never a bare number without decomposition" is independently re-derived three times** (`docs/sprint-6/24-confidence-model.md`'s confidence bands, `docs/sprint-6/15-evidence-engine.md`'s evidence quality dimensions, and `docs/company/12-founder-dashboard.md`'s rejection of a composite index) — good, since it means the principle is genuinely load-bearing rather than stated once and forgotten, but it has never been named as its own standalone principle anywhere. It should be — see `17-company-dna.md`.
- **"Something is deferred to its own, later, dedicated review" appears as a pattern at least five separate times** across the outcome-intelligence, marketplace, and knowledge-graph documents. This is good discipline exercised consistently, but the *pattern itself* — "high-trust-risk work gets its own gate, never rides along" — has never been elevated to a named operating principle the way "trust before monetization" was in `08-founding-culture.md`. It's arguably the more precise, more actionable version of that principle and deserves to replace or sharpen it.

## Missed opportunities

- **Nothing in this document set discusses what happens if a design partner says no, or churns.** Every document assumes forward progress. A real strategy document set for a company built to last 20 years should have at least one paragraph on what a failed pilot teaches, and how that's distinguished from "the wedge is wrong" versus "this specific account was wrong" — currently absent entirely.
- **No document addresses talent/hiring risk directly** beyond `09-operating-model.md`'s function list. Given "Marvin" (per this session's own context) appears to be operating close to solo with AI assistance, the single largest execution risk to everything in this document set — the founder's own bandwidth and the difficulty of hiring the first non-founder decision-maker into a pre-revenue, high-conceptual-density company — is under-addressed. Taken up directly in `20-investor-critique.md`.
